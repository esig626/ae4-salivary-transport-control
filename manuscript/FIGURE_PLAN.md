# Figure-led article plan

The paper should be edited around the figures, not by accumulating every task chronologically. The current five-figure sequence is the proposed main story.

## Figure 1: From phenotype reproduction to mechanism discrimination

**Question:** What changed between the 2018 model and the present reconstruction?

**Story:** The old model had the correct biological actor but a minimal acid–base architecture and no NBC. The present study separates wild-type reconstruction, blinded mechanism discrimination and target-selected construction.

**Main text sections:** Introduction; model overview.

## Figure 2: NBC closes the acid–base balance

**Question:** Why was NBC needed?

**Story:** Substantial productive AE4 chloride loading exports too much base for the old NHE1/AE2/CO2 architecture. Stimulus-recruited inward Na/2HCO3 transport supplies the missing alkalinity. The repaired wild type remains physiological and places approximately 20% of positive chloride loading through AE4.

**Panels:** intracellular pH and bicarbonate; NBC, AE4 and NHE1 fluxes; integrated chloride-loading partition.

**Main text sections:** hidden alkalinity constraint; wild-type validation.

## Figure 3: Equal cation routing does not recover the knockout phenotype

**Question:** Does the original cation-balance explanation survive?

**Story:** No. Equal routing gives only a 3–4% loss because NKCC1 compensates. Chloride falls, but sodium does not remain elevated. The algebraic cancellation of explicit AE4 terms from sustained CaCC balance explains why cation split alone has little control.

**Panels:** cumulative secretion; intracellular chloride; intracellular sodium.

**Main text sections:** Task 40; balance identity.

## Figure 4: Catalán 2025 mechanism-class discrimination

**Question:** Can experimentally motivated AE4 transport classes explain the gland phenotype without added coupling?

**Story:** None does. The NaCl-in/KHCO3-out class increases secretion after deletion because compensation is enormous. The K879 bicarbonate class gives almost no phenotype. The largest valid loss is small and thermodynamically opposed. Carbonate classes fail the wild-type pH gate, making pH a mechanism discriminator.

**Panels:** null secretion loss; NKCC1 compensation; affinity and pH failures.

**Main text sections:** Task 42 and post-reveal interpretation.

## Figure 5: Constructive inverse comparison

**Question:** What additional coupling is mathematically capable of generating the observed magnitude?

**Story:** Strong AE4-dependent CaCC recruitment can generate a 30% null deficit and retain chloride, but it is target-selected, leaves sodium below WT and gives the wrong temporal pattern. It identifies a class of missing network coupling rather than a discovered mechanism.

**Panels:** Task 40 versus Task 41 magnitude; early-to-late temporal comparison; null Na and Cl trajectories.

**Main text sections:** constructive model; limitations; experimental predictions.
