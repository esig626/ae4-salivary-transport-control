# Task 47: dynamic reconstruction of the AE4–NKCC1–K-recycling bottleneck

## Objective

Determine whether the substantial AE4-loss secretion phenotype can arise from a **physiologically defensible dynamic limit on ion recycling**, specifically the coupled block

`AE4 <-> NKCC1 <-> intracellular K <-> K channels <-> membrane potential <-> Na/K-ATPase`,

without fitting a transporter cap or any other parameter to the approximately 35% AE4-null secretion deficit.

The purpose is not to create another mechanism family. It is to test the single mechanistic lead produced by the completed CBM work and either establish it or reject it cleanly.

## Central structural identity to test dynamically

For the frozen canonical CBM,

`K_efflux = J/3 + NKCC1 + NHE1/3 - AE4/2`.

For AE4 knockout,

`K_efflux_KO = J/3 + NKCC1 + NHE1/3 >= J/3`.

Interpretation: deleting AE4 removes a term that lowers the K-recycling requirement, while any additional NKCC1 recruitment used to rescue chloride loading adds a one-for-one K-recycling demand.

This identity is a **handoff clue**, not a new hard constraint to impose on the ODE model. The dynamic model must determine whether the required K recycling is actually feasible once electrochemical driving force, concentrations, voltage, conductance and pump kinetics are respected.

## Scientific question

Answer one question only:

> Does the existing or minimally corrected dynamic K-recycling system impose a real physiological ceiling on NKCC1-mediated compensation after AE4 deletion?

If yes, identify the smallest mechanistic correction needed and demonstrate the causal chain. If no, reject this hypothesis and state exactly why.

## Prohibited shortcuts

Do **not**:

- fit any parameter to the AE4-null secretion deficit;
- choose a K conductance, pump capacity, NKCC capacity or voltage dependence because it produces approximately 35%;
- invent a numerical WT/KO NKCC equality or percentage tolerance from the Peña-Münzenmayer assay;
- perform a Cartesian-product parameter sweep;
- introduce multiple new mechanism classes;
- search combinations of arbitrary caps;
- change calcium signalling, CaCC recruitment or unrelated transport blocks unless a demonstrated equation-level inconsistency requires it;
- use the 5% AE4 trajectory or null phenotype as an optimisation target;
- silently alter the frozen WT reference model.

The phenotype may be used only as a final validation after the reconstruction is fixed from independent physiology, conservation, thermodynamics and existing provenance.

## Phase 1: audit the existing dynamic recycling block

Before changing code, reconstruct the exact equations and units for:

1. NKCC1 Na/K/Cl flux and its thermodynamic driving force;
2. apical and basolateral K-channel currents/fluxes;
3. intracellular K conservation;
4. membrane-potential equation and all K-current contributions;
5. apical and basolateral Na/K-ATPase fluxes and saturation/capacity;
6. intracellular Na coupling generated by NKCC1 and cleared by the pump;
7. any conductance partition assumptions between apical and basolateral K routes.

For every parameter in this block, classify it as measured, literature-derived, inherited model value, calibrated value, placeholder, or unconstrained effective parameter. Reuse Task 43 provenance where available rather than relabelling old values as measurements.

Check signs, stoichiometry, dimensions and conservation independently.

## Phase 2: quantify dynamic headroom without phenotype fitting

Using the frozen WT parameterisation, quantify during the relevant stimulation window for WT and AE4-null:

- NKCC1 flux;
- total K influx through NKCC1;
- apical and basolateral K efflux;
- total K-recycling flux;
- intracellular K concentration;
- membrane potential;
- Na/K-ATPase flux and fractional utilisation of its available kinetic range;
- electrochemical K driving force for each K channel;
- K-channel current relative to the conductance-limited current available at the instantaneous state;
- intracellular Na and its pump burden;
- chloride secretion/output.

The key diagnostic is whether the current model allows AE4-null compensation because K channels and/or pump have unrealistically large dynamic reserve, or whether those components remain comfortably physiological even during rescue.

Where possible, derive local sensitivities or analytical bounds instead of brute-force sweeps.

## Phase 3: minimal reconstruction, only if justified

Only if Phase 1 or Phase 2 exposes a concrete physiological defect, make the smallest correction needed to represent the missing constraint.

Examples of admissible corrections, **only when independently justified**, include:

- correcting a K-channel electrochemical driving-force law;
- restoring a missing concentration or voltage dependence;
- correcting channel localisation or stoichiometric bookkeeping;
- replacing an effectively unlimited conductance/pump representation with a literature-supported kinetic law;
- correcting a parameter that Task 43 shows was a placeholder or misinterpreted capacity.

Do not add a phenomenological `K_max`, `NKCC_max`, genotype multiplier, or secretion penalty merely to generate the desired phenotype.

If the existing equations are already physiologically correct and the hypothesis fails, stop and report failure. That is a valid scientific result.

## Final validation

After the pre-phenotype checkpoint is remote, compare the fixed prediction with the held-out AE4-loss phenotype and the 5% AE4 condition.

Report separately:

1. whether the direction of the phenotype is recovered;
2. the predicted null cumulative secretion deficit;
3. the predicted 5% AE4 deficit;
4. whether NKCC1 compensation is reduced for the correct mechanistic reason;
5. whether intracellular Na, K, Cl, pH, volume and voltage remain physiologically admissible;
6. whether WT remains acceptable;
7. whether any remaining discrepancy points to a different missing mechanism.

Do not retune after reveal.
