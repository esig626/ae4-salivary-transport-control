### Exact chloride-difference balance

For genotype `g`, intracellular chloride amount obeys

`x'_g = 2N_g + A_g + E_g - J_g`,

where `J_g` is total apical chloride export.

For WT minus AE4 KO, with `A_KO=0`:

`delta' = 2(N_W-N_K) + A_W + (E_W-E_K) - (J_W-J_K)`.

Under the central experimentally constrained shared-supply idealisation `N_W=N_K` and `E_W=E_K`:

`delta' = A_W - DeltaJ`.

Hence exactly

`Integral DeltaJ dt = delta(0) + Integral A_W dt - delta(T)`.

The final-state reservoir difference must be reported; the initial reservoir is not automatically assumed to be completely secreted.

### Source-scale sufficiency calculation

Using the pre-Palk Task37 cell-volume scale `1.448426 pL`, the measured resting chloride difference represents approximately

`(50.10 - 36.50) * 1.448426 = 19.70 fmol`

more intracellular chloride in WT at stimulation onset.

The pre-Palk Task37 WT trajectory gives approximately `41.25 fmol` of WT AE4 chloride loading over 0–600 s on its saved 60 s grid and approximately `225.70 fmol` of apical chloride export.

Therefore the maximum reservoir-plus-AE4 chloride advantage is approximately

`19.70 + 41.25 = 60.95 fmol`,

about `27.0%` of that WT apical-export scale before the final reservoir term is subtracted.

The accepted Task41/50 target-calibrated construction produced its approximately 30% fluid phenotype with a `26.88%` fall in null CaCC chloride export. The scale agreement is not validation, but it establishes that the experimentally measured chloride-reservoir difference plus ordinary WT AE4 supply is large enough in principle to account for the missing export without a genotype-dependent channel multiplier.

### Electrochemical consequence of measured low chloride

For an apical chloride channel

`E_Cl = V_T ln([Cl]_i/[Cl]_l)`.

Thus the measured `50.1 -> 36.5 mM` WT-to-KO chloride change shifts chloride reversal by

`V_T ln(36.5/50.1) = -8.4646 mV` at 37 C.

This shift is independent of lumen chloride.

In the frozen Task40 resting electrical context, zero outward chloride driving occurs at approximately `45.0 mM` intracellular chloride. The measured WT value is above that model threshold and the measured KO value below it. This is a model-context diagnostic, not a measured lumen claim.

Linearising the exact two-membrane current closure gives

`d(E_Cl - V_a)/dE_Cl = alpha > 0`

for positive stable membrane/paracellular differential conductances. Therefore voltage relaxation may attenuate but cannot reverse the effect: lower intracellular chloride necessarily reduces outward chloride driving.

### Comparison theorem for the reduced common-supply model

If WT and KO have the same NKCC/AE2 supply and the same apical-channel law, and total apical chloride export `J(x,t)` increases monotonically with intracellular chloride amount, then

`delta' = A_W - [J(x_W,t)-J(x_K,t)]`.

With measured `delta(0)>0` and `A_W>=0`, `delta` cannot cross from positive to negative. Therefore

`x_W(t) >= x_K(t)` and `J_W(t) >= J_K(t)`

for the reduced common-supply system.

The desired genotype direction therefore follows without an AE4-dependent channel multiplier.

### Why shared beta/IPR conductance can amplify AE4 dependence

Locally, `DeltaJ approximately kappa(t) delta` with `kappa>0`. Then

`delta' = A_W - kappa delta`.

Increasing a **shared** beta/IPR apical conductance increases `kappa`, discharges the WT-versus-KO chloride-reservoir difference faster, and increases cumulative export separation through the exact finite-window identity. Thus Catalán's beta pathway can expose the AE4 supply deficit even if its conductance is identical in both genotypes.

The beta pathway is therefore reinterpreted as increased chloride **demand**, while AE4 provides chloride **supply/reserve**.
